## 1. Shared Clock and Data

The **Inter-Integrated Circuit (I²C)** bus uses serial data **SDA** and serial clock **SCL**. Open-drain outputs pull a line low. Pull-up resistors produce the high level.

A controller starts a transaction and sends an address. The addressed target acknowledges supported transfers.

## 2. Transfer Sequence

1. A **START** occurs when SDA falls while SCL is high.
2. The controller sends the address and direction bit.
3. Each byte has eight bits, followed by an acknowledgment clock.
4. A **STOP** occurs when SDA rises while SCL is high.

Data remains stable while SCL is high, except for START and STOP conditions. A repeated START can retain control between transaction phases.

Distinguish a 7-bit device address from an address byte that includes the direction bit.

## 4. Worked Example

**Assumptions:** Supply is 3.3 V. Bus capacitance is 200 pF. Required rise time is at most 300 ns.

Assume each device supports 3 mA at a low voltage of 0.4 V.

1. Minimum resistance is approximately **967 Ω**.
2. Maximum resistance is approximately **1770 Ω**.
3. A nominal **1.5 kΩ** resistor gives approximately **254 ns** rise time.
4. At 0.4 V low, resistor current is approximately **1.93 mA**.

Include resistor tolerance and capacitance uncertainty. Parallel pull-ups on multiple boards reduce the effective resistance.

## 5. Fault Checks

Check address conflicts, clock stretching, startup states, and stuck-low recovery. A controller that ignores clock stretching can violate a target's timing.

Measure rise time at the most heavily loaded point. Confirm both high and low voltages.
